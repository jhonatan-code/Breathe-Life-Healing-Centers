Nicotine is the part of cigarettes that hooks us: a mild mood-boosting rush that keeps us coming back. Over time, the brain adjusts to a steady supply and becomes physically dependent on it. Quit cold turkey and remove that supply, and the brain reacts, often with:

- Cravings
- Increased appetite
- Depression
- Restlessness
- Difficulty concentrating
- Irritability
- Disturbed sleep
- Light-headedness
- A wet, mucosal cough
- Weight gain
- Constipation
- Dry mouth

## Four ways to manage it

There are four main approaches: quitting technique, nicotine replacement therapy, medication, and natural remedies.

### Quitting technique

Quitting all tobacco cold turkey tends to bring withdrawal symptoms on quickly, and they can last weeks or months. Tapering down over time works better for some people. Setting a smoking limit that decreases daily or weekly is one approach, and there are mobile apps built specifically to help plan a taper.

### Nicotine replacement therapy

E-cigarettes, patches, gum, and other tobacco-free nicotine products let you taper the withdrawal itself over time while cutting out the more than 70 carcinogenic chemicals found in tobacco smoke.

### Medication

Two prescription medications are commonly used to help manage withdrawal while quitting: Chantix (varenicline) and Zyban (bupropion). Clinical research comparing the two has found meaningfully higher quit rates with Chantix, followed by Zyban, both well above placebo, though your prescriber is the right person to talk through actual numbers and what fits your situation.

Chantix acts directly on the brain's nicotine receptors, reducing both the severity of withdrawal and the pleasurable reward of smoking itself. It can also come with side effects, most notably psychological changes like irritability, sleeplessness, and mood changes, sometimes serious enough that people switch to a different approach. Zyban was originally developed as an antidepressant before its usefulness for smoking cessation was discovered. It tends to have fewer side effects than Chantix but is somewhat less effective on its own, which is why it's often paired with nicotine replacement therapy.

### Natural remedies

If quitting cold turkey without patches or medication is the plan, these can help:

1. **Reach for fruit or vegetables when a craving hits.** Carrots, grapes, apples, nuts, whatever you actually enjoy. Stepping outside for fresh air while you snack makes it even more effective.
2. **Skip the alcohol for now.** Cravings intensify the more you drink. Once withdrawal eases in a month or two, alcohol becomes far less of a trigger.
3. **Build a bedtime ritual.** Good sleep hygiene, winding down with consistent cues that tell your body it's time to rest, makes a real difference. A bath, candles, soft music, or essential oils can all work. Give it a week or two of consistency before expecting results.
4. **Move more.** Extra exercise in the first month or two after quitting helps with depression, cravings, irritability, restlessness, and sleep, and it also helps offset the weight gain that often comes with quitting. Plan for it rather than being surprised by it.
5. **Carry a reusable water bottle.** More water gives your hands and mouth something to do instead of reaching for a cigarette, and it helps with constipation, dry mouth, and weight gain too.
6. **Pick up something new.** Early on, it's easy to think about cigarettes constantly. A new hobby or interest gives your attention somewhere else to go, and by the time the cravings fade, you might have a genuinely new skill to show for it.
