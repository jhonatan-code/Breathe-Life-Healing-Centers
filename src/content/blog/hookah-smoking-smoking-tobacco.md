Hookah smoking is a common social activity, often considered a low-risk alternative to smoking cigarettes. That reputation doesn't hold up. Even a single night of hookah smoking can carry real health consequences.

According to the Mayo Clinic, tobacco smoke isn't any less toxic when it passes through a hookah pipe than it is in a cigarette. Because the water in the pipe doesn't filter it out, a hookah smoker may actually inhale more tobacco overall. A typical hookah session runs around 60 minutes, far longer than it takes to smoke a cigarette, so the combination of more tobacco and a longer session adds up to more exposure, not less.

Regular hookah use is linked to a higher risk of lung cancer, oral cancer, and heart disease, and it can lead to tobacco dependence like any other form of smoking. Secondhand exposure carries its own risks, and use during pregnancy has been linked to lower birth weight.

What about someone who only smokes hookah occasionally, on a Saturday night out? A study published in the journal *Cancer Epidemiology, Biomarkers & Prevention*, reported by the Huffington Post, found that a single evening of hookah smoking can spike nicotine levels in urine by more than 70 times and increase markers linked to cancer risk. Even occasional use carries measurable effects.

Doing something in moderation is less harmful than doing it regularly, and most people who use hookah occasionally aren't going to be seriously harmed by one evening. But as someone in recovery and someone who works in addiction treatment, moderation isn't something everyone can rely on. Whether or not that's true for you, it's worth knowing the real health risks of the things we do for entertainment. An occasional Saturday night probably won't catch up with you right away, but over a lifetime, it can add up. Every activity has a price, and it isn't always a financial one.
