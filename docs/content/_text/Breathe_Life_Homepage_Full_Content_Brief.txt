WHY BREATHE — SIX THINGS THAT MAKE US DIFFERENT
This section does not exist on the live homepage today. It should. This is the entire strategic basis of the brand and currently appears nowhere a visitor can see it in under 30 seconds.
1. Staff who've lived it
Every person here, from the front desk to clinical leadership, has personal recovery experience. You're not being treated by someone who studied your experience from the outside.
2. A real chemsex treatment track
Crystal meth, GHB, and other substances used in sexual contexts are treated here as their own clinical issue — not a moral failing, not folded into general sex addiction treatment. One of the only programs in the country built specifically around this.
3. Sexual health as part of care
STI testing, HIV status support, hormone continuity, and PrEP-aware language — built into treatment, not bolted on.
4. Gender-affirming care, woven in
Correct names, correct pronouns, gender-appropriate facilities, and a clinical team trained in the minority stress model — the default here, not a specialty add-on.
5. Chosen family, welcomed
Family therapy doesn't assume blood relationships. Partners, chosen family, and community are valid participants in your recovery.
6. The hard stuff, taken seriously
Religious trauma, discrimination, minority stress, and collective grief are treated with the same clinical weight as any other presenting issue — never background noise.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Homepage FAQ module — in addition to the per-page FAQ sections already confirmed sitewide. These should be the highest-intent, most differentiating questions, not generic rehab FAQs.
Is Breathe Life only for LGBTQ+ people?
No. We're built for the LGBTQ+ community first, and that shapes everything about how we treat people — but our doors are open to anyone whose values align with how we operate.
What is chemsex, and how is it different from what other rehabs treat?
Chemsex is the use of substances like crystal meth, GHB, or mephedrone in sexual contexts — common in gay and queer men's communities and rarely addressed as its own clinical issue. We treat it as exactly that: not a moral failing, and not the same thing as sex addiction.
Do you accept my insurance?
Most likely, yes. We're in-network with most major providers. Our team verifies your specific benefits before you commit to anything — usually within minutes.
Will my information be confidential?
Yes. Your privacy is protected under federal law, and we never share your information without your consent.
Can my family be involved in my treatment?
Yes — and we don't assume family means blood relatives. Partners, chosen family, and close community are welcome participants in our Family Program.
What if I've tried treatment before and it didn't work?
That doesn't disqualify you from anything here. A lot of the people we treat have tried before. That's part of the story, not a reason to turn you away.
Do you offer gender-affirming care during treatment?
Yes — correct names, correct pronouns, gender-appropriate facilities, and clinical staff trained specifically in gender-affirming practice. It's built into the program, not an add-on.
How long does treatment take?
It depends on the level of care and your individual needs. Most stays start with a 30-day minimum and are shaped around your progress, not a fixed calendar.
What happens after I finish treatment?
Our Alumni Program includes ongoing support groups and community — recovery doesn't end at discharge, and neither do we.
