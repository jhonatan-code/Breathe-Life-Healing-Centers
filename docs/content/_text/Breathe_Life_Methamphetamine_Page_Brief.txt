UNDERSTANDING METH ADDICTION
Meth changes more than behavior. It can affect sleep, thinking, mood, trust, relationships, and how safe a person feels in their own body. For some people it gets tied to performance and staying on. For others it's about escape, loneliness, or a mind that won't slow down.
Methamphetamine use carries real physical and psychiatric risk, including cardiovascular strain, severe sleep deprivation, and episodes of paranoia or psychosis. That's part of why medical support during early recovery matters, not just willpower.
Common signs of meth addiction
	•	Using more, or more often, to get the same effect
	•	Extended periods without sleep, followed by crashes
	•	Paranoia, agitation, or disconnection from reality
	•	Weight loss, skin issues, or changes in appearance
	•	Secrecy around use, especially where it intersects with sex or intimacy
This page also covers amphetamine addiction more broadly. The patterns and treatment approach are closely related, so we address both here rather than splitting into separate pages.
WHEN METH USE IS TIED TO SEX, INTIMACY, OR IDENTITY
This is more common than most people expect, and it deserves care that doesn't flinch from it. When meth use is connected to sex, secrecy, or compulsive patterns, generic addiction treatment often misses what's actually going on.
Crystal Clear, our program built specifically for this, treats it as the real clinical issue it is. Not a moral failing, and not folded into general sex addiction treatment.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
I've relapsed before. Can you still help?
Yes. A long history with meth or a past relapse isn't proof recovery is out of reach. It's usually a sign someone needed a deeper level of support the first time.
What if my meth use is tied to sex or something I feel ashamed to talk about?
That's more common than people think, and it's exactly what Crystal Clear was built for. You won't be the first person to bring this into the room.
Can a family member reach out first, if I'm not ready?
Yes. Many people who contact us are a parent, partner, or close friend calling on behalf of someone they love. We can talk you through where to start.
Is meth withdrawal medically dangerous?
It can involve serious psychiatric symptoms like paranoia or psychosis, along with physical strain and severe sleep disruption. Medical monitoring during early recovery matters.
Does insurance cover meth addiction treatment?
In most cases, yes. We're in-network with most major providers and verify your specific benefits before you commit to anything. We do not currently accept Medi-Cal or Medicare.
Is this treatment only for LGBTQ+ clients?
No. We're built for the LGBTQ+ community first, and that shapes how we treat everyone, but our doors are open to anyone whose values align with how we operate.
