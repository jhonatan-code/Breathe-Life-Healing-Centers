UNDERSTANDING ALCOHOL ADDICTION
Alcohol is legal, everywhere, and often the substance people take most seriously last — which is part of what makes it dangerous. It's also one of the few substances where withdrawal itself can be medically dangerous, not just uncomfortable. If you've tried to stop before and your body fought back, that's not a personal failure. That's withdrawal, and it's exactly why medical detox exists.
Common signs of alcohol addiction
	•	Needing more to feel the same effect
	•	Drinking to manage anxiety, shame, or a body that doesn't feel safe
	•	Withdrawal symptoms — shaking, sweating, nausea, anxiety — when you try to stop
	•	Missing work, relationships, or commitments because of drinking or recovering from it
	•	Repeated attempts to cut back that haven't stuck
ALCOHOL USE IN THE LGBTQ+ COMMUNITY
This isn't a coincidence, and it isn't a character flaw. National survey data (SAMHSA) has consistently found LGBTQ+ adults report meaningfully higher rates of alcohol and substance use than heterosexual adults — a pattern researchers connect to minority stress, discrimination, and a lack of care that actually understands the difference. We built this program around that reality, not around pretending it doesn't exist.

FREQUENTLY ASKED QUESTIONS
Is alcohol withdrawal actually dangerous?
For some people, yes — it can include seizures and other serious symptoms. That's why medical detox, supervised around the clock, is the safe starting point rather than trying to stop on your own.
Do you offer day treatment, or only residential?
Both. We offer medically supervised day detox and PHP/IOP options for people who don't need or want a residential stay, alongside full residential care for those who do.
Is this program only for LGBTQ+ clients?
No. We're built for the LGBTQ+ community first — that shapes how we treat everyone — but our doors are open to anyone whose values align with how we operate.
Does insurance cover alcohol detox and treatment?
In most cases, yes. We're in-network with most major providers, and our team verifies your specific benefits before you commit to anything.
What if I've relapsed before?
That doesn't disqualify you. Many of the people we treat have tried before — that's part of the story, not a reason to turn you away.
Will you address my mental health, not just the drinking?
Yes. Depression, anxiety, and trauma are treated as part of the same plan through our dual diagnosis approach — not as a separate, secondary concern.
