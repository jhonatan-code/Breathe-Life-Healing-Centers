## 1. How You Help People Get Better

**1.1 Clinical approach for someone never in treatment**
> RA: I think Breathe gets to the root issue. Oftentimes other treatment centers treat the symptoms or the presenting issue, but I think Breathe looks at what's underneath the problem and treats that.

> CW: The clinical approach is to offer compassion, inclusion and safety.

> Unsigned: I would describe Breathe's clinical approach as a comprehensive, trauma-informed, and client-centered approach that addresses the underlying emotional and psychological factors contributing to addiction, rather than focusing solely on stopping substance use.

**1.2 First few days**
> Unsigned: During the first few days at Breathe, our priority is to help clients stabilize physically and emotionally while beginning to establish a sense of safety, trust, and connection.

**1.4 Therapies used most / any not to list**
> RA: I use CBT, DBT, MI, and IFS mostly.

> CW: IFS uses parts work to allow one to find out that there is more to them than just their identified "issues." DBT provides tangible tools they can use in real time for emotional regulation. CBT challenges their negative thoughts and finds a new perspective. Motivational interviewing helps one to find their voice. Polyvagal and somatic practices helps someone regulate their emotions and/or be aware of when it's time to pause, etc.

> Unsigned: At Breathe, we use an integrative, individualized approach that draws on evidence-based therapies and relationally focused interventions. Our work often incorporates Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (CBT), Dialectical Behavior Therapy (DBT), trauma-informed care, motivational interviewing, and attachment- and relationally focused therapy. We also integrate psychoeducation, mindfulness, emotional regulation, and relapse-prevention strategies.

**1.5 Substance use with depression, anxiety or trauma**
> RA: I focus on building safety and trust, identifying triggers and patterns, developing healthy coping skills, and using evidence-based interventions.

> CW: The client will see the psychiatrist to see if they need medication. All of the tools we use here treat all of these. Mindful practices help with depression. Polyvagal and Somatic practices help with anxiety. IFS and DBT are great for trauma, along with our spiritual and experiential groups.

> Unsigned: At Breathe, we recognize that substance use rarely exists in isolation. Depression, anxiety, trauma, and difficulties with emotional regulation can contribute to addictive behaviors, while substance use can further intensify these challenges. Our approach is to treat the whole person rather than focusing exclusively on the substance use itself.

## 2. Levels of Care and What Comes Next

**2.1 PHP and IOP schedule at 8060, who it fits**
> RA: PHP is 6 hours a day, 5 days a week, and IOP is 3 hours a day, 5 days a week. It's a good fit for people that need a structured, supportive level of care.

> CW: PHP is M-F 9:00am to 4:15pm. IOP5 if M-F from 9:00am to 12:15pm. IOP3 is 3 days a week from 9:00am to 12:15pm. **2.2 Coming from a hospital or another program**
> RA: We collaborate with the referring provider or program to support a smooth transition. We review the needs, treatment history, medications, and goals so we can provide continuity of care.

> CW: The client that steps into PHP from an outside program is giving an orientation, introduced into their first group upon arrival and assigned a peer buddy.

> Unsigned: When someone comes to Breathe from a hospital or another treatment program, we work to make the transition as seamless and clinically appropriate as possible. We begin by coordinating with the referring team to understand the client's treatment history, current clinical needs, progress, and any ongoing medical or psychiatric concerns. With the appropriate releases of information, we communicate with the hospital, previous treatment providers, and other members of the care team to support continuity of care.

**2.3 Preparing for life after treatment**
> RA: I focus on building skills for maintaining recovery outside of treatment, including relapse prevention, coping skills, emotional regulation, healthy relationships, and connection. I also work with clients to develop aftercare plans and appropriate referrals.

> CW: We help set up clients with a sober living, an outside therapist and psychiatrist, alumni offerings and anything else that is needed. Each person's needs are different. Some may need to transfer to a program with case management and life skills. Others may need a sober companion.

## 4. LGBTQ+ Affirming Care

**4.1 Day to day**
> RA: LGBTQ+ affirming care means creating an environment where clients can be fully themselves without having to educate or defend their identity. We use clients' names and pronouns, recognize their unique experiences, and provide care that is respectful, inclusive, and individualized.

> CW: Using correct pronouns. Being supportive of client's medical appointments. Encouraging that their attire, hair, nails, etc. is affirming to who they are and their choices.

> Unsigned: At Breathe, LGBTQ+ affirming care is not simply a philosophy we talk about; it is something we strive to put into practice every day. It begins with creating an environment where clients can show up as they are, without fear of judgment, discrimination, or having to explain or defend their identity.

We use your name and pronouns, honor your identity, and recognize that your gender identity is not something that needs to be explained, questioned, or changed. Our goal is to create a space where you can focus on your recovery and feel safe and supported.

At Breathe, our goal is to create a space where clients can focus on their recovery without feeling that they need to defend their identity or educate their treatment team about who they are.

## 5. Chemsex Programming

**5.1 Explaining it to someone new to the term**
> RA: Chemsex is the use of substances in connection with sexual experiences, which can sometimes create unique challenges around substance use, intimacy, relationships, sexual health, and emotional well-being. Our program provides a nonjudgmental space to explore those experiences while addressing substance use and supporting healthier, more fulfilling relationships with sex, intimacy, and self.

> CW: It is term used to describe using a chemical component with sexual encounters. They become intertwined in the brain.

> Unsigned: At Breathe, our Chemsex programming is designed for individuals who struggle with the use of substances to enhance, prolong, or intensify sexual experiences. Chemsex can involve substances such as methamphetamine, GHB/GBL, and other drugs, often in the context of prolonged sexual encounters or group sex. Over time, what may begin as a way to experience pleasure, confidence, connection, or freedom can become a compulsive pattern that affects a person's physical health, emotional well-being, relationships, and ability to experience intimacy without substances.
>
> What makes our approach distinctive is that we don't treat the substance use in isolation from the sexual and emotional experiences connected to it. We recognize that, for some individuals, Chemsex may become a way of managing loneliness, shame, trauma, internalized stigma, or difficulties with intimacy and vulnerability. For others, it may be connected to the pursuit of validation, belonging, excitement, or an idealized version of themselves.
>
> Our programming creates a safe, affirming, and nonjudgmental space where clients can openly explore these experiences without feeling shamed for their sexuality or sexual behaviors. We help clients understand the relationship between substances, sexual arousal, fantasy, compulsivity, and emotional regulation, while developing healthier ways to experience pleasure, connection, and intimacy.
>
> Ultimately, our goal is not simply to help clients stop using substances. It is to help them understand what Chemsex has come to represent in their lives, address the underlying patterns that sustain the behavior, and build a fulfilling recovery that includes a healthier relationship with their sexuality, themselves, and others.

**5.2 Typical week**
> RA: A typical week combines individual therapy, group therapy, psychoeducation, and recovery-focused work for the intersection of substance use, sexuality, intimacy, and relationships.

> CW: 2 chemsex groups plus one love and sex specialty group.

> Unsigned: Psychoeducational groups and intimacy disorder individual therapy
