## 1. How You Help People Get Better

**1.1 Clinical approach for someone never in treatment**
> Meet them where they are, build trust, educate with a gentle spirit, work with them to face the fears, deal with any shame, and recognize the power of change.

**1.2 First few days (detox, assessment, treatment plan)**
> The client is acclimated, greeted, introduced to the program by clinicians, staff, and peers, gradually integrated into the community, and helped to understand and trust the purpose of their presence, as well as their therapist, to begin the process.

**1.3 Typical day in residential**
> Awakening, medication, time to use the gym, meditate, read, prepare for the day, attend groups, and/or see their therapist and/or case manager, or attend any necessary appointment, observe free time after groups, be part of a community meeting, enjoy dinner, and either spend some time in community or at a meeting. The day ends with medications and lights out.

**1.4 Therapies used most / any not to list**
> Residential works with CBT, DBT, Motivational interviewing, mindfulness, wellness groups, and psychoeducation. Clients attend process groups, yoga, breathwork, sound baths, skills-based groups, and psychoeducation that covers extensive preparation work. Residential is where the client gets back into their body and is readied to move into the deeper trauma work that is to come.

**1.5 Substance use with depression, anxiety or trauma**
> At the residential level, you help them feel heard, stay regulated, and understand that the work is largely suggestive and based on experience and education. If you push too hard with new clients at first, the result could be negative, but it is also important to build the trust relationship that helps the client hear what they need, while maintaining boundaries that help them learn to share what is necessary honestly. The therapist should not be a friend that can be manipulated.

**2.2 Coming from a hospital or another program**
> A transfer is handled the same way as any client coming to Breathe. The client completes an intake, is introduced to the program, and at residential, they meet with the nurses first. Staff will check their bags, settle them into their groups, and, depending on the client's condition, they will either rest or integrate into the community or groups at an individual pace.

**2.3 Preparing for life after treatment**
> I believe it is important to slowly integrate changes and coping skills throughout the programming. At residential, this is less prevalent than in PHP and definitely at IOP, where more life skills and time management will come into play.

## 3. Families

**3.1 Parent or partner calling for someone not ready**
> Throughout the programming, the therapist's job is to be honest with the family and loved ones. This doesn't have to be blunt, especially in the early days when a client is likely to resist the idea of change and treatment. In residential especially a client is going to experience a grieving period about a lifestyle and sometimes the drugs themselves that they are looking to stop using. In residential, it is also important to remember that the client is going to be in post-acute withdrawal syndrome, which will affect how they feel and act about themselves and where they are.

_Note: "lifestyle" here refers to drug use, not LGBTQ+ identity; still, the site rule bans the word in LGBTQ+ context, so don't quote it._

**3.2 Family program, who can join**
> The family program is reserved for clients in PHP or IOP. Any family is potentially able to be a part of this, but it will require therapist approval and will be motivated by the client's and family's willingness and whether or not everyone is available to do this in person.

## 5. Chemsex Programming

**5.1 Explaining it to someone new to the term**
> Breathe Life Healing Center's Chemsex Program is a specialized, multidisciplinary intervention designed to address the physical, psychological, and social challenges associated with the use of drugs such as methamphetamine in sexualized contexts, commonly referred to as "chemsex."
>
> Our program combines evidence-based medical, spiritual, and psychological strategies to support individuals engaging in chemsex, helping them reduce risks, manage dependencies, redirect thinking, and improve overall well-being. Breathe and the ChemSex program aim to meet participants where they are in their journey, offering compassionate, nonjudgmental, sex positive care tailored to their unique needs.

**5.2 Typical week**
> ChemSex clients are provided with three designated ChemSex groups per week at all levels of care. They are given our Crystal Clear and Sexually Recovered workbook (written by key staff), and they are put on a caseload with a CSAT therapist. They are afforded specialized work to meet their needs while also being integrated into the wider community.
